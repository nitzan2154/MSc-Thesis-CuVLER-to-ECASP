\chapter{Conclusions and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented ECASP, a silhouette-based approach for calibrated multi-view 6DoF pose estimation under occlusion.
By using only binary masks and camera calibration, ECASP removes dependence on RGB appearance and depth sensing while preserving the geometric constraints of calibrated views.
ECASP combines geometry-aware cross-view fusion with hybrid learned--geometric pose estimation.

Trained only on CAD-rendered synthetic masks, ECASP achieves accurate pose estimation on real multi-view surgical-object data despite using substantially weaker visual-input assumptions than RGB- and RGB-D-based methods.
Evaluation with YOLO masks shows that ECASP remains effective with automatically extracted, imperfect masks, while silhouette refinement recovers substantial accuracy lost to segmentation errors.
These results suggest that calibrated multi-view silhouettes provide a practical representation for reducing appearance-related synthetic-to-real dependence in occluded settings.

\textbf{Limitations.} ECASP assumes accurate camera calibration, reliable binary masks, and known rigid objects with CAD models.
Calibration errors can misalign epipolar sampling and triangulation, while fragmented or missing masks can degrade silhouette features and pose estimation.
Our predicted-mask evaluation confirms this sensitivity, particularly for thin object parts, although mask-specific training and silhouette refinement can improve robustness.
These assumptions limit applicability in settings without reliable masks, object models, or calibrated multi-view observations.
